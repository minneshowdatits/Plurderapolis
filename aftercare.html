Aftercare & Recovery

The show ends when the lights come on. Recovery is the 48 hours after, and doing it well is the difference between bouncing back and losing your whole week.

The short version

  • Sleep is the #1 recovery tool. Protect it like the headliner.
  • Eat real food. Drink water and electrolytes, not just one or the other.
  • Give your brain 48 hours before you judge your life, your relationships, or your choices.
  • Check on your friends. Let them check on you. Tuesday texts save people.

1 · Body

The first 48 hours: body first

Your body just ran a marathon in a loud, hot room on no sleep. Treat recovery like an athlete would:

  • Sleep. Not "lie in bed scrolling": actual sleep, in a dark cool room. If your brain won't shut off, that's normal; give it time, not more stimulation.
  • Eat. Real food with protein and carbs, not just snacks. Your body burned through its reserves. Restock them.
  • Hydrate smart. Water plus electrolytes (sports drinks, coconut water, electrolyte packets). Plain water alone after heavy sweating doesn't replace what you lost.
  • Sunlight and gentle movement. A short walk outside does more for a comedown than anything in a bottle. Fresh air, daylight, easy pace.
  • Skip the "hair of the dog." Drinking to take the edge off just borrows tomorrow's wellbeing and hands you the bill with interest.
1 · Body

The 48-hour rule

For two full days after a big night: no major decisions, no doom-scrolling your ex, no quitting anything, no deciding you're a failure. Comedown feelings are weather, not climate. They pass. Judge your life with a rested brain or don't judge it at all.

2 · Crew

Check-in culture

The group chat going quiet after a big weekend is normal: everyone's recovering. But a quick "how's everyone feeling today?" on Tuesday costs you ten seconds and can mean everything to the person who's been spiraling alone since Sunday.

  • Normalize post-show check-ins in your crew. Make it a ritual, not an intervention.
  • If someone goes quiet, reach out directly, not with "are you okay??" panic, just "hey, thinking of you."
  • Let people check on you too. "I'm fine" when you're not helps nobody.
  • Buddy system works after the show, not just during it.

2 · Crew

Integration (for the psychonauts)

If your weekend involved psychedelics, the real work starts after. Insights fade fast: write them down within 24 hours, even if they're messy or half-formed. Voice notes count.

Capture it now: before it fades

Dump it here, messy and unedited. What came up: feelings, memories, realizations, weird imagery. This stays on your device, nothing is sent anywhere.

  • Journal what came up: feelings, memories, realizations, weird imagery. Don't edit, just capture.
  • Talk it through with someone you trust, ideally someone who's been there.
  • Give big realizations time before acting on them. The urge to overhaul your life at 4 AM usually looks different on Thursday.
  • If a trip stirred up something heavy that won't settle (old trauma, persistent anxiety, dark thoughts), that's worth bringing to a therapist, not just sitting with. See our mental health page.

3 · Mind

Making it sustainable

The best aftercare is needing less of it. The ravers who are still going hard at 35 aren't tougher than you: they're just better at recovery:

  • Space it out. Rest weekends between party weekends. Your brain and body need off-seasons.
  • Protect your sleep like a ritual, not an afterthought.
  • Keep non-scene joys alive: hobbies, daylight friendships, things that don't start at 10 PM.
  • Notice the creep. If "just this weekend" becomes every weekend becomes Wednesday, that's data, not failure.
