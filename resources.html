Minnesota law, in plain words

3 topics

Calling for help: Minnesota’s Good Samaritan law (Minn. Stat. 604A.05) protects a person who seeks medical help for someone who may be overdosing, and the person overdosing, from being charged or prosecuted for personal possession of a controlled substance. The person overdosing is also protected for possessing drug paraphernalia. It does not stop police from making an arrest, and it does not cover alcohol offences.

Naloxone: Anyone may carry and give naloxone in Minnesota (Minn. Stat. 604A.04). Several naloxone nasal sprays are approved for over the counter sale, including Narcan 4 mg, RiVive 3 mg, and Rextovy 4 mg. Other naloxone formulations remain prescription only. Naloxone is free at syringe service programs, naloxone access points, and community organisations. At a pharmacy it can cost money.

Supplies and testing: Possessing drug paraphernalia is not a state crime; the old law (Minn. Stat. 152.092) was repealed on August 1, 2023, products that detect fentanyl are expressly not drug paraphernalia (Minn. Stat. 152.01, subd. 18), and drug checking equipment, including xylazine test strips, was decriminalised in the same 2023 laws. State law does not override city ordinances here (Minn. Stat. 152.205), so local rules can differ. Intentionally manufacturing paraphernalia for delivery is still an offence. Pharmacies may sell syringes without a prescription, with no state quantity limit (Minn. Stat. 151.40).

Law wording checked against the Minnesota Revisor of Statutes on November 1, 2026.
