Safe Rides Home

Getting home is part of the night. How to plan the ride before you go out, check you are getting into the right car, use the safety tools built into the apps, and get out and get help if a ride feels wrong.

Rideshare Safety, Start to Finish

1Plan the Ride Home Before You Go Out
  • Decide how you are getting home before the first drink or dose. Clear plans are much harder to make at the end of the night than at the start.
  • Charge your phone and bring a backup battery if you can. A dead phone means no ride, no map, and no way to call for help.
  • Set up the safety tools while you are clear headed: trusted contacts, trip sharing, and PIN verification in Uber or Lyft. They take two minutes now and are hard to find in a panic.
  • If you are a woman or nonbinary and Lyft shows Women+ Connect in your app, turning it on raises your chances of matching with women and nonbinary drivers. It is a preference, not a guarantee, so every check on this page still applies.
  • Save your home address, or a nearby cross street if you do not want your exact address in the app, so you are not typing it in a loud parking lot.
  • Tell a friend the plan: where you are going, who you are with, and roughly when you expect to be home. Agree on a check in text for when you get in.
  • Keep a backup: a second rideshare app, a taxi number saved in your phone, or a friend who agreed ahead of time to be the emergency call.
2Order Smart and Wait Safe
  • Wait inside the venue, or in a lit spot with other people around, until the app says your driver is actually arriving. Do not stand alone on the curb with your phone out longer than you need to.
  • Check the pickup pin on the map. If a driver asks you to walk somewhere dark or out of the way to meet them, cancel and order again.
  • Ride with a friend when you can, and keep your group together at pickup so nobody gets left deciding alone.
  • If someone in your group is very intoxicated, do not put them in a stranger’s car alone. Ride with them, or hand them to a trusted sober person who is staying with them.
  • Never accept a ride from someone who pulls up and calls out that they are your driver. Real rides are booked and tracked inside the app, full stop.
3Check Your Ride, Every Time
  • Match all four before you open the door: license plate, make and model, car color, and the driver’s name and photo in the app. If one does not match, it is not your ride.
  • Ask “Who are you here for?” and let the driver say your name first. Never lead with “Are you here for me?” Anyone can say yes to that, including someone who is not a driver at all.
  • If PIN verification is on, the driver needs your PIN before the trip can start. It is one more check that you and the car in the app are matched.
  • Sit in the back seat, on the passenger side when you can: it gives you more distance and a door on either side.
  • Before the car moves, make sure your door opens from the inside. Check that the child safety lock on your rear door is off, and try the handle if you are not sure.
  • If anything does not match, do not get in. Walk back inside, cancel in the app, and report it. Refusing the wrong car is never the rude move.
4During the Ride
  • Buckle up, back seat too.
  • Share your trip with a trusted contact and keep location sharing on. In Uber that is Share My Trip; Lyft has trip sharing in its safety tools.
  • Keep your phone out and follow along on your own map. Speak up early if the route drifts away from what the app shows.
  • Answer the app’s check ins. Uber RideCheck and Lyft Smart Trip Check In flag unexpected stops and route changes and ask if you are okay. That prompt is there for exactly the moment you might need it.
  • Keep your bag and phone with you in the back seat, not in the trunk.
  • You do not owe anyone conversation or personal information. You do not have to share where you live, where you work, or who you are seeing. Lie if you have to, and say things like “my friend is tracking me” or “my roommate is expecting me.” Sound known and expected.
5If a Ride Feels Wrong
  • Trust your gut. A wrong feeling is reason enough. You do not need proof, and you do not owe anyone a polite explanation.
  • Ask the driver to stop somewhere public and well lit, like a gas station, a store, anywhere with people around, and get out. You can sort the app and the fare out from safety.
  • Use the emergency help inside the app. Uber's emergency button calls 911 and shares your location. Lyft's emergency help, through ADT, can send help or connect you to 911, with a silent option if speaking is unsafe.
  • Can't speak safely? Minnesota has Text to 911 statewide. Text your location and what the emergency is in plain words, then answer the dispatcher's texts. The state's rule: call if you can, text if you can't.
  • If you cannot get out, call 911 and stay on the line. Say where you are and where the car is headed.
  • Do not argue about the route or accuse the driver while the car is moving. Getting out and getting somewhere safe is the whole job.
6After the Ride, and Walking to Your Car
  • Send the “home safe” text you promised. Your person cannot relax until it lands.
  • Rate and report anything that felt off, even small things. Reports are how patterns get seen.
  • If you parked yourself: try to park in the same section or side of the lot every time, so you never have to wander or guess where your car is.
  • Have your keys in your hand before you leave the building, so you can get in and lock up right away.
  • Avoid getting gas late at night when you can plan around it.
  • If you feel uneasy or someone approaches you, hit your car alarm. It is loud, it draws attention, and it says back off.

Other Safe Ways Home

Riding With Friends, or Driving Yourself
  • Pick the sober driver before anyone starts, and treat it as settled. No renegotiating at the end of the night.
  • Never get in a car with a driver who has been drinking or using, even for a short ride. Short rides crash too. Order a car or call someone instead.
  • If a friend who has been drinking or using insists on driving, offer the easy alternative out loud: “I already ordered us a car.” Getting their keys matters less than getting them another way home.
  • Sober drivers: if your passengers are in rough shape, walk them to their door when you can, and wait until they are inside before you leave.
Transit, Taxis, and the Last Stretch on Foot
  • Metro Transit: check the last bus or train time before you go out, not when you are already outside. Late night service is thinner than people expect.
  • Want a person to help plan the trip? Metro Transit’s text line for transit information is 612 444 1161 during their open hours.
  • On transit, sit near the front or near other riders, keep your belongings on you, and use Metro Transit Text for Safety, 612 900 0411, to report anything that feels off. It is listed again in the resources below.
  • Taxis: use a licensed cab, or have venue staff call one for you, and confirm the meter or the fare before you set off.
  • Walking the last stretch: stay on lit streets, keep your location shared with your person, and stay on the phone with them if a stretch feels empty or wrong.
Beating Surge Pricing
  • Compare both apps and a taxi before you accept a surge price. On the biggest nights, a taxi meter can beat surge.
  • For a planned night, book a scheduled ride earlier in the day instead of ordering into the bar close rush.
  • Wait it out somewhere safe and warm for ten or fifteen minutes. Surge usually drops once the first wave of riders clears.
  • Walk a block or two away from the venue to a calmer street. Pickups are faster there, and sometimes cheaper outside the busiest zone.
  • Split the fare with your group inside the app rather than one person eating the surge alone.
  • Ride transit for the first stretch and order the car from a quieter stop further out.

❄️ Winter Ride Rules

For Minnesota winters, checked November 2026. Cold changes the ride home math: waits get longer, batteries die faster, and standing outside becomes its own risk.

Cold Kills Phone Batteries
  • Keep your phone in an inside pocket against your body, not an outer coat pocket and not in your hand. Cold drains batteries fast, and a dead phone means no ride, no map, and no way to call for help.
  • Carry a small backup battery on winter nights out, and drop your screen brightness while you wait.
  • Order the ride before you step out of the heated building whenever you can.
  • If your battery is already low, tell your person your plan out loud and early, while the phone still works.
Order Early, Wait Inside
  • In snow, drivers are scarcer and trips take longer. Order earlier than feels necessary.
  • Stay inside until the app shows the car actually close. Curb time in deep cold is the part to shrink.
  • Tell the driver what you are wearing ("red coat, front door") so they spot you fast.
  • Set your pin at the actual door you will exit, not the middle of the block.
On Snow and Ice
  • Never pressure a driver to hurry on snow or ice. A slower ride beats a crash.
  • If your pickup spot is an unplowed mess, walk carefully to the nearest cleared corner and message the driver the change.
  • Every matching rule still applies in a storm. Bad weather is not a reason to get into an unmatched car.

🏟️ Venue Pickup Spots

Only one big Twin Cities venue actually publishes rideshare pickup guidance, and it is marked below. Everything else here is a general move built on the venue address and its nearest transit, checked November 2026. The pattern everywhere: pick a lit main street corner a block off the door crush, set your pin there, and walk to it.

The Armory 🚗 Official guidance

500 S 6th St, Minneapolis. The venue itself recommends Portland Ave for taxi, Uber, Lyft, and drop off. Set your pin on Portland Ave S near S 6th St instead of loading on S 6th St in front of the doors.

🚈 Government Plaza and U.S. Bank Stadium stations, Blue and Green lines, one on either side of the venue.

First Avenue & 7th St Entry

701 N 1st Ave, Minneapolis. Meet the car on 1st Ave N at 8th St N, one block south of the door crush, right by the surface lot.

🚈 Warehouse District / Hennepin Ave Station, Blue and Green lines, about two blocks away.

Fine Line

318 N 1st Ave, Minneapolis. Meet on N 1st Ave at 3rd St N, the main Warehouse District corner at the venue block, rather than mid block in front of the door.

🚈 Warehouse District / Hennepin Ave Station, Blue and Green lines, about two blocks away.

The Fillmore Minneapolis

525 5th St N, Minneapolis. Meet just off 5th St N around 3rd Ave N by the Target Field Station area, a landmark both sides can find, instead of asking a driver to stop on 5th St where the trains run.

🚈 Target Field Station, Blue and Green lines, right at the venue.

Skyway Theatre

711 Hennepin Ave, Minneapolis. Skip a curbside stop on Hennepin at 7th at show out, the busiest corner in the area. Walk one block to 1st Ave N at 7th St N, or south on Hennepin to 8th St.

🚈 Warehouse District / Hennepin Ave Station, Blue and Green lines, about two blocks north.

Palace Theatre

17 W 7th Pl, St Paul. Meet on W 7th St at St Peter St or Wabasha St, the main street ends of the short theatre block, which a driver can find and stop at far more easily than 7th Place itself.

🚈 Central Station, Green line, about three blocks east.

Amsterdam Bar and Hall

6 W 6th St, St Paul. Meet at Wabasha St N and W 6th St, the venue's own corner, rather than mid block on 6th.

🚈 Central Station, Green line, about two blocks east.

Turf Club

1601 University Ave W, St Paul. University and Snelling is a big lit landmark but very busy, so set the pin just off it: Sherburne Ave behind the building, or University Ave at Pascal St, one block west.

🚈 Snelling Avenue Station, Green line and A Line, right at the corner.

Varsity Theater

1308 SE 4th St, Minneapolis. Meet at SE 4th St and 14th or 15th Ave SE, a block or two from the Dinkytown door crowd, or walk south to the wider Washington Ave SE frontage.

🚈 East Bank Station, Green line; the E Line stops at University Ave and 15th Ave SE, about two blocks east.

Uptown Theatre

2900 Hennepin Ave, Minneapolis. Hennepin and Lake is the obvious landmark and the busiest junction in the area, so set the pin a block off it, at W 31st St to the south or W 28th St to the north.

🚌 No rail here. B Line stations at Lake St and at Lagoon Ave on Hennepin, and the E Line at Uptown Transit Station.

Myth Live

3090 Southlawn Dr, Maplewood. Set the pin at County Rd D and Southlawn Dr, the main lit intersection by the front lot, or at the Maplewood Mall frontage, so the car stays out of the front lot exit crush.

🚌 Maplewood Mall Transit Center, next door in the mall complex, with routes 54 and 64 running to downtown St Paul.

Safety Resources

🌆 Minnesota / Minneapolis Resources
Our Streets Minneapolis

Advocacy and resources for safe, accessible streets, including info relevant for late-night walkers. They work to make streets places where people can easily and comfortably walk, bike, roll, and use public transit.
https://ourstreetsmpls.org

Metro Transit – Rider Safety

Safety tools, bus/rail security presence, and how to report harassment or unsafe situations on public transit in the Twin Cities. Metro Transit has a Safety & Security Action Plan with more than 40 actions to improve safety.
https://www.metrotransit.org/rider-safety

MN Dept. of Public Safety: Text to 911

Statewide service for when a voice call is not safe, and a first contact option if you are deaf, deafblind, hard of hearing, or speech impaired. Call if you can, text if you can’t: text your location and what the emergency is, in plain words.
https://dps.mn.gov/divisions/ecn/911-program/text-911

MN Coalition Against Sexual Assault (MNCASA)

Statewide support, survivor resources, and prevention programs, including safety strategies that apply to nightlife and rideshare.
https://www.mncasa.org/

MN Day One Crisis Line

24/7 statewide crisis line run by Cornerstone. Links you to local shelters, advocates, or transportation help.
1-866-223-1111 | https://cornerstonemn.org/

Tubman

Minneapolis-based crisis line, advocacy, and safe transport assistance for people leaving unsafe situations.
https://www.tubman.org/

Minneapolis Pedestrian Safety

The city builds safer street crossings with features like bump outs, medians, ramps, and flashing lights to improve pedestrian safety.
https://www.minneapolismn.gov/pedestrian-safety
