Something going wrong?

1 · Do this first

How to respond to an overdose
  1. Call 911 immediately. Say the person is unresponsive and describe any substances if you know them. Stay on the line.
  2. Check responsiveness and breathing. Shout their name, shake shoulders hard. Watch the chest: slow, shallow, stopped, gurgling, or snoring while unconscious all count as red flags.
  3. Give naloxone (Narcan). Nasal spray: peel the package, place the tip in one nostril, press the plunger firmly (4 mg, one full spray). No improvement in 2–3 minutes? Give a second dose in the other nostril.
  4. Rescue breathing / CPR if they are not breathing and you know how.
  5. Roll them into recovery position and stay with them until medics arrive. Naloxone wears off in 30–90 minutes, so they can slip back into overdose after it fades.
2 · Keeps the airway open

Recovery position

Anyone unconscious but breathing goes here. It keeps the airway open if they vomit.

  • Lay the person on their side
  • Bend the top knee to keep them stable
  • Tilt the head back slightly to open the airway
  • Make sure the mouth is clear
  • Stay with them and keep watching their breathing
3 · Recognize

How to spot an overdose

Opioids (fentanyl, heroin, oxycodone)

  • Pinpoint pupils
  • Slow or no breathing (fewer than 8 breaths a minute)
  • Blue lips or nails
  • Gurgling or snoring sounds while unconscious
  • Unresponsive or very hard to wake
  • Limp body, clammy or pale skin

Stimulants (MDMA, meth, cocaine)

  • Overheating, agitation, confusion, hallucinations
  • Fast heartbeat, high blood pressure, chest pain
  • Seizures or collapse

Alcohol poisoning

  • Vomiting while unconscious
  • Cold, pale, or bluish skin
  • Slow or irregular breathing
  • Seizure or unresponsiveness

Other drugs and polydrug use

  • Benzodiazepines: heavy sleepiness, slurred speech, unresponsiveness
  • Combinations are unpredictable. Treat any emergency as serious and call 911.
4 · Carry it

Naloxone (Narcan): carry it, use it
  • Carry at least 2 doses per person. It is free in Minnesota.
  • Store at room temperature, away from sun and extreme cold.
  • Check expiration dates regularly.
  • Nasal spray: peel, place tip in a nostril, press firmly. Call 911. Repeat in 2–3 minutes if needed.
  • Teach your buddies the recovery position and to keep watching the person until help arrives.
  • Our harm reduction booths carry free Narcan plus instructions, test strips, and water. Find a booth
5 · Cool down

Heat and hydration emergencies
  • Sip water steadily. Small amounts often beat chugging.
  • Use electrolytes if sweating heavily.
  • Move to shade or air conditioning.
  • Cool with wet towels or ice packs on the neck, wrists, and armpits.
  • Confusion, dizziness, or collapse from heat: call 911. If someone is confused or unconscious from heat, do not give them fluids (choking risk). Cool them and wait for medics.
6 · Breathe through it

Panic and anxiety
  • Racing heart, shaking, sweating, shortness of breath: this can be panic, and panic passes.
  • Grounding, 5-4-3-2-1: name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste.
  • Breathing: inhale 4 seconds, hold 4, exhale 6. Repeat.
  • Move somewhere calm and quieter.
  • Reassure them: "You are safe. This will pass."
  • Minnesota Warmline: 651-288-0400, or text "Support" to 85511.
7 · Protect the head

Seizures
  • Clear the area and cushion their head.
  • Turn them on their side if you can.
  • Do NOT put anything in their mouth.
  • Call 911 if the seizure lasts more than 5 minutes, if another follows, or if they do not wake up after.
8 · Rare but urgent

Serotonin syndrome
  • High fever, confusion, muscle rigidity, sweating, fast heartbeat.
  • Often triggered by MDMA combined with antidepressants or several serotonergic drugs at once.
  • Cool them down and call 911 immediately. This is a medical emergency.
9 · At the venue

Crowd and venue safety
  • Use the buddy system and set a meetup point before the show.
  • Stay at the edges of dense crowds. Do not push forward.
  • If someone falls, make space around them and alert staff.
  • Know where the exits and first aid stations are when you arrive.
  • Keep your phone charged or bring a power bank.
10 · Everything else

Other emergencies

Choking

  • Abdominal thrusts (Heimlich) if they are awake. If they pass out, call 911 and start CPR.

Allergic reaction

  • Swelling, hives, trouble breathing: use an EpiPen if available and call 911.
11 · Before you go out

Be prepared

What to carry

  • Naloxone (free in MN, carry 2 doses)
  • Electrolytes and water
  • Fentanyl and xylazine test strips (how to use them)
  • Emergency contacts written down, ID, condoms

Calling 911

MN Statute 604A.05 protects you when seeking help during an overdose.

Overdose: 5 steps

  1. Call 911. Say "unresponsive" + your location.
  2. Check breathing. Slow, stopped, gurgling = red flag.
  3. Narcan nasal spray: one full spray (4 mg) in a nostril. Repeat in 2–3 min if needed.
  4. Rescue breathing / CPR if not breathing.
  5. Recovery position. Stay until medics arrive (Narcan wears off in 30–90 min).

Recovery position

  • On their side, top knee bent, head tilted back, mouth clear.

Overdose signs

  • Opioids: pinpoint pupils, slow/no breathing, blue lips, gurgling, limp.
  • Stimulants: overheating, agitation, chest pain, seizure, collapse.
  • Alcohol: vomiting while out, cold/pale skin, slow breathing.

Heat emergency

  • Shade, water sips, cool neck/wrists/armpits. Confusion or collapse: call 911, no fluids if unconscious.

Seizure

  • Clear area, cushion head, side position, nothing in mouth. 911 if over 5 min.
