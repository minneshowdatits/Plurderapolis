Symptom Detective

Someone looks wrong and you are not sure what is happening. Answer a few quick questions and get clear action steps plus a 911 script.

What it does

Helps tell overdose, heat stroke, and alcohol poisoning apart from the signs you can actually see.

When to use it

When someone at an event looks unwell. Run it before you call 911 so you have the details ready.

Why it helps

You get action steps and a ready to read 911 script, so the call goes faster when seconds count.

6 quick questions. Takes about a minute. Not a diagnosis, just guidance.

Good to know

Before you start

  • If they are unresponsive or barely breathing, skip the quiz and call 911 now. You can always come back.
  • Answer from what you can see, not what you assume they took.
  • When in doubt between two results, treat it as the more dangerous one.
  • Minnesota's Good Samaritan law (604A.05) gives limited immunity to people who call 911 during an overdose.

Naloxone access in Minnesota

Naloxone is available without a prescription at Minnesota pharmacies under the state's standing order. You can also get free naloxone from the Department of Health distribution programs.

Heat illness prevention

Heat illness is common at summer events. Early signs: excessive sweating, weakness, nausea, headache. Move to shade, hydrate, cool with wet cloths.

CDC heat safety
Source: Centers for Disease Control

Substance testing resources

Some harm reduction organizations provide substance checking. Always test before use and never use alone. Know what you are taking and start with a small amount.

DanceSafe resources
Source: DanceSafe.org

Harm reduction support

Trained harm reduction volunteers work many events. Look for orgs providing water, cool spaces, and non-judgmental support. The Never Use Alone hotline offers confidential support.
