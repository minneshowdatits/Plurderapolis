Good to know

Before you start

If they are unresponsive or barely breathing, skip the quiz and call 911 now. You can always come back. Minnesota Good Samaritan law 604A.05 gives limited immunity to people who call 911 during an overdose.

Naloxone in Minnesota

Naloxone is available without a prescription at Minnesota pharmacies under the state standing order, and free through Minnesota Department of Health distribution programs. It cannot hurt someone who is not on opioids.

Source: Minnesota Department of Health

When naloxone is not enough

Xylazine and medetomidine are sedatives turning up mixed into fentanyl. Naloxone does not reverse them, so a person may keep sleeping even after naloxone works on the opioid. Give naloxone anyway, keep giving rescue breaths, and stay with them.

Source: CDC xylazine guidance, and CDC Health Advisory on medetomidine, April 2, 2026

Heat can kill on its own

Heat stroke is not just being too warm. Confusion, hot dry skin, or passing out in the heat means call 911 and cool the person fast while you wait.

Source: CDC Heat Related Illness guidance

Sources used for this update

  • CDC, What You Should Know About Xylazine: give naloxone anyway, rescue breaths are especially important, recovery position.
  • CDC Health Alert Network, April 2, 2026: medetomidine in the US illegal fentanyl supply, profound sedation and slow heart rate, naloxone still advised because fentanyl is usually involved.
  • CDC, Heat Related Illnesses: heat stroke signs include high body temperature, hot red dry or damp skin, fast strong pulse, confusion, and losing consciousness. Do not give the person anything to drink.
  • American Heart Association and American Red Cross first aid guidance, 2024: rapid active cooling for heat stroke, cold water immersion where it is available and safe.
  • National Harm Reduction Coalition, Responding to Stimulant Overamping: cool with ice packs, mist and fan, wet cloths under armpits and on forehead, water or electrolyte drinks for dehydration.
  • FDA naloxone labeling: repeat a new dose every 2 to 3 minutes if the person does not respond or breathing slows again.
  • Content is based on established harm reduction resources and clinical literature. Checked October 2026. Links added October 2026.
