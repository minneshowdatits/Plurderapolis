🎃 Halloween show prep

  • Build the costume over warm layers. Fishnets alone at 2 AM in a parking lot is a bad time.
  • Plan the coat check. Know where it is before doors, and keep your ticket somewhere you will not lose it.
  • Bring the crew kit: Narcan, fentanyl test strips, earplugs, a portable charger.
  • Watch your drink at house parties and afterparties. If it tastes off or you feel wrong fast, tell someone immediately. Drink spiking guide
More Halloween specifics
  • Face paint and masks can overheat you on a packed floor. If you feel dizzy, step out and cool down.
  • Props that look like weapons will get you turned away at the door. Leave them home.
  • Agree on a meetup spot with your crew in case phones die.

🥶 Cold nights: the line is the danger zone

  • Layer like this: moisture wicking base, warm middle, windproof outer. Cotton holds sweat and makes you colder.
  • Hypothermia signs: violent shivering, confusion, slurred speech, drowsiness. Get inside and warm up slowly.
  • Frostbite signs: numb, white or grayish skin on fingers, toes, ears, nose. Do not rub it. Warm gently.
  • Phone batteries die fast in the cold. Keep your phone close to your body, and screenshot your ride info.
The sweat trap

The classic fall mistake: overdress, sweat through your base layer dancing, then freeze in the damp clothes on the way out. Vent your shell on the floor and change into something dry if you soaked through.

🤧 Respiratory virus season is here

  • October is the ideal flu shot month according to the MN Dept. of Health. Last season flu hospitalized 5,500+ Minnesotans and 369 died.
  • Updated COVID vaccines are available now at MN clinics and pharmacies. Extra important if you are 65+, under 2, pregnant, or have a health condition.
  • RSV vaccine: recommended for adults 75+ and for ages 50 to 74 with heart, lung, or immune risk factors. Best taken August through October.
  • Sick? Sit the show out. One packed floor can take out a whole crew for two weeks. Mask up in crowds if you are sniffly but going anyway.
Why this matters for ravers

Hours of heavy breathing in a packed, poorly ventilated room is about as efficient a virus delivery system as exists. Getting vaccinated in October means peak protection lands right in the January and February show stretch.

🌙 Dark evenings are coming

  • Clocks fall back November 1. Sunset is already 6:21 PM and shrinking. Plan for full dark on the walk to and from the venue.
  • Stick to lit routes and share your live location with a friend. Ride safety guide
  • Prebook your ride home before you go in. Surge pricing at 2 AM is how bad decisions happen.

🧠 The mood dip is real

  • Shorter days mess with sleep and mood for a lot of people. If the comedown feels heavier than it used to, that is worth noticing.
  • Light in the morning, movement, and keeping one sober anchor night a week help more than people expect.
  • Struggling? Mental health resources and the panic toolkit are there any time.

❄️ Winter: indoor season survival

  • Snow driving: give yourself double the travel time, keep a blanket and charger in the car, and never let the tank drop below half on show nights.
  • Transit delays are normal. Have a backup ride plan before you leave the house.
  • Holiday party season means more drinking around more substances. Check every combo in the interaction checker before you mix.
  • New Year's Eve: the biggest amateur night of the year. Eat, hydrate, pace, and keep Narcan on you.
  • Deep winter mood: the January and February stretch is the hardest. Keep your people close and check the mental health page.

🌱 Spring: thaw season

  • Festival announcements start dropping. Budget early, buy from official sellers only, and screenshot every confirmation.
  • Mud season is real. Outdoor spring shows mean waterproof boots, not your good sneakers.
  • Allergy season overlaps with spring shows. Know which allergy meds do not mix with what you take. Rx combo checker
  • Prom and graduation parties: younger crowds, less experience, higher risk. Share the Start Here guide with anyone new.

Full spring guide drops in March.

☀️ Summer: festival season

  • Heat is the number one killer at summer festivals. Water, shade breaks, and electrolytes are non negotiable.
  • Sunscreen and a hat. Sunburn plus dehydration ruins day two before it starts.
  • Storms roll in fast on the plains. Know the festival evacuation plan when you arrive, not when the sky turns green.
  • Test everything. Summer means new connects and festival dealers. Test strip guide
