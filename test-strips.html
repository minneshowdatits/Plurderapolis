Test strips: know before you go

Fentanyl and xylazine test strips are cheap, legal in Minnesota, and take 5 minutes. Here is exactly how to use them. Timer included.

The basics

Two strips, two jobs

🧪 Fentanyl strips

Detect fentanyl and many of its analogs in powders, pills, and residue. The most common strip out there.

🧫 Xylazine strips

Detect xylazine ("tranq"), increasingly found in the Minnesota supply. A separate strip. Fentanyl strips do not reliably catch it.

Neither strip catches everything else that might be in the bag. A strip is one tool, not a guarantee. Keep reading for the limits.

1 · Mix

Mix it right

1

Sample + water in a clean container

Put at least 10mg in, about enough to cover Lincoln's hair on a penny. Add water and mix well:

  • Meth or MDMA: 1 teaspoon of water per 10mg
  • Everything else: ½ teaspoon of water per 10mg

Stimulants need more water or the strip can read wrong. And mix thoroughly. Fentanyl does not spread evenly, so the bit you test has to represent the whole bag.

2 · Dip

Dip the strip

2

15 seconds, wavy end down

Hold the solid end. Dip the wavy end into the liquid up to the MAX line, not past it. Hold for about 15 seconds.

3 · Wait

Lay flat, start the timer

3

Flat surface, 5 minutes

Set the strip on something clean and flat, then start the timer. Read it right at 5 minutes. Not before, and don't trust it after 10.

5:00
Time's up. Read your strip now.

Your phone will buzz when the 5 minutes are up (if vibration is supported).

4 · Read

Tap what you see

Heads up: strips read backwards from pregnancy or COVID tests. Tap the result that matches your strip:

Negative: no fentanyl detected

Two lines means the strip did not detect fentanyl in your sample, even if the second line is faint. Still: it only tested the bit you mixed, and it says nothing about xylazine or anything else. Stay cautious.

Positive: fentanyl present

One line means fentanyl was detected. Treat the whole batch as contaminated.

Safest move is not using it. If you do: don't use alone, have naloxone ready and someone nearby who knows the signs. See our overdose page.

Invalid: test again

No control line (or no lines at all) means the test didn't work. Use a new strip and start over. Never reuse a strip.

What strips can't do

  • No amount info. A strip says present or not present, never how much.
  • Negative is not a green light. It only tested the sample you mixed, and mixing matters.
  • Won't catch everything. Fentanyl strips miss xylazine and won't reliably catch everything else in the supply. That's what the xylazine strip is for.
  • Won't confirm LSD or rule out NBOMe. These strips answer a different question. For blotter sold as acid, an Ehrlich reagent test is the relevant check, and a bitter tasting tab is not LSD. More in our psychedelics guide.

Legal in Minnesota

Test strips are legal to possess in Minnesota. They are not considered drug paraphernalia under state law. Carrying them can't get you in trouble.

Get free strips from the orgs on our resources page. Many mail them or hand them out at events, including our booth.
